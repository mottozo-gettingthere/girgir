IDLE

Open a channel

One of you creates the channel and shows a code. The other scans it, then shows a code back. Two scans and you are linked.

Both phones must be on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot.

Setting up

First, put it on both home screens

Do this once, while you still have internet. After that it runs with no connection at all.

On Android

Open this page in Chrome and tap Install at the top. If it is not there, open Chrome's three-dot menu and pick Add to Home screen.

On iPhone

Open this page in Safari, tap Share (the square with an arrow out of it), scroll down, then Add to Home Screen.

Open it once from the home screen while you still have internet, so it can store itself. After that it needs none.

  1. 1 · Android makes the network

    Turn on the Android phone's hotspot and join it from the iPhone. It needs no internet — it is only there to carry your voices.

    Set the band to 2.4 GHz, and turn off "switch hotspot off when no devices are connected". On the iPhone, turn off Wi-Fi Assist.

  2. 2 · Pair with two scans

    One taps Create channel and holds up the code. The other taps Join channel, scans it, and holds up the reply. Or both tap Create channel and point the phones at each other — they sort out between themselves which one hosts.

    The first phone keeps its camera on behind its own code, so there is nothing to press in between — just turn it towards the reply and it links itself.

    Turn both screens up bright. If a camera will not cooperate, open "Use text" under the code and paste it across instead.

  3. 3 · A pair each

    Give each phone its own pair of earphones. Simpler than splitting one pair, and it works with any model, AirPods included — you each hear and speak through your own set.

    Rather hold the phone to your ear? On the iPhone that is already what happens: call audio goes to the earpiece. Android always uses the loudspeaker and a web app cannot switch it.

  4. 4 · Pick how you talk

    Open keeps the microphone live — best with earbuds. Latch goes live on a tap and stops on the next. Hold is live only while pressed, which is what you want on a speaker.

    You each choose your own mode. They do not have to match.

What the readouts mean

Bars, top right
Link quality, worked out from round-trip time, packet loss and jitter. Four bars is a clean link.
LINK
Round-trip time and the share of audio packets being lost.
BATT / THEIRS
Battery on each phone. The iPhone cannot report its own level to a web app, so it shows a dash there.
INPUT
Which microphone is actually open — EARBUD, WIRED or BUILT-IN — so a headset that failed to connect is obvious rather than silent.
NET
The network you share, and each phone's last number. No browser can read a Wi-Fi name, so this is how you confirm you are on the same one. Amber means different networks, and audio will not flow.
OUT
Where their voice comes out. A picker appears where the phone allows one; otherwise it names what the system does — earpiece on iPhone, loudspeaker on Android.
Meters
Live audio level — yours in amber, theirs in teal. Set a name in settings and the labels use it instead of YOU and THEM.

Storage on this phone

Only the app itself is cached, so it runs offline. There are no logs, no call history and no recordings. Two things are remembered between launches — the name you chose and the fact that you installed it — and nothing else survives. Anything an older version left behind is wiped at startup.

Settings

NAME

Shown on the other phone instead of THEM. Leave it empty and the plain labels are used.

MIC 100

How loudly you are sent. Push past 100 for a quiet headset; too far and you will distort.

VOL 100

Use the phone's volume buttons to set the incoming level.

OUT —

Half duplex mutes the other person while you transmit, so you never hear yourself back. Roger beep sounds a short tone when they stop talking. Both start on.

Keep screen on holds the screen awake while you are linked. Turn it off to pocket the phone — on Android the channel keeps running with the screen off, best in OPEN mode. An iPhone suspends the page either way, so it has to stay awake there.
